In September 2025, OpenAI announced five new sites at once. Together with Abilene and ongoing CoreWeave projects, [Stargate reached nearly 7 GW of planned capacity](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/).

Eleven months later, OpenAI signed one PORTS-Pike agreement for 8 IT-GW.

The previous round spread capacity across Texas, New Mexico, Wisconsin, and Ohio before it approached 7 GW. This time, one Ohio campus held 8 GW. Either data centers had suddenly learned to replicate ten times faster, or PORTS-Pike was never an enlarged version of the earlier campuses.

The answer sits inside the land's previous job.

<!-- more -->

## Is PORTS-Pike Really Larger Than the Previous Six?

The comparison needs a boundary first. The earlier “nearly 7 GW” combined planned capacity across six Stargate campuses and related projects. PORTS-Pike discloses 8 IT-GW, the power available to servers, networking, and storage. Individual site figures may represent critical IT load, power supply, or generation. They cannot be added into one precise total.

![The Scale of PORTS-Pike Versus the Previous Stargate Round](/images/ports-pike-scale-comparison.en.svg)

The imperfect denominator does not remove the scale conflict. Eight IT-GW is roughly 4.2 times OpenAI's [total available compute in 2025](https://openai.com/index/a-business-that-scales-with-the-value-of-intelligence/) and 27% of its 30 GW target for 2030. A capacity target that previously belonged to a portfolio now sits inside one campus agreement.

The useful question is not whether eight is exactly one more than seven. It is: **what kind of campus can hold capacity that used to require a portfolio?**

## Is PORTS-Pike Still a Data Center?

The [Permitting Council defines it](https://www.permitting.gov/newsroom/press-releases/ports-technology-campus-latest-gain-fast-41-coverage) not as one building but as an infrastructure project combining a frontier training AI data center with new power generation. The [PORTS-Pike website](https://portscampus.com/) gives it a still larger boundary: a 10 GW data center complex, 10 GW of new generation, 765 kV transmission, private and remediated DOE land, and NVIDIA compute.

The earlier Stargate campuses mostly began with a site question: where does enough power already exist, and how quickly can a facility deliver a first batch? PORTS-Pike changes the delivery unit. **Instead of waiting for the grid to reveal 8 GW of spare capacity, the program builds generation, transmission, and the data center together.**

It is closer to an industrial base whose final product is AI compute. The 8 GW does not belong to one building. It is a six-year program ceiling assembled from multiple parcels, buildings, substations, and cluster batches.

An Ohio campus can push its boundary this far for a reason more specific than empty land.

## Why Piketon?

In 1952, the U.S. Atomic Energy Commission selected Piketon for the Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant. DOE's reasons sound much like an AI-campus shortlist today: a large flat site, abundant water, labor, transportation, and above all reliable electrical power.

![From Uranium Enrichment to AI Compute, Piketon Becomes a Strategic Power Site Twice](/images/ports-pike-industrial-reuse.en.svg)

Gaseous diffusion consumed extraordinary amounts of electricity. [Portsmouth's historical record](https://portsvirtualmuseum.org/history/uranium-enrichment.html) says the 1956 supply agreement called for continuous delivery of 1,950 MW. Two dedicated plants generated the electricity, and two double-circuit 330 kV lines carried it into the site. The power alone slightly exceeded OpenAI's 1.9 GW of available compute in 2025.

After uranium enrichment stopped in 2001, the site did not leave behind a factory ready to accept GPUs. It left an industrial location previously organized for a national, power-intensive mission: more than 3,700 acres, high-voltage corridors, water, transportation, skilled labor, and federal land still undergoing remediation. [DOE's site background](https://www.energy.gov/pppo/portsmouth-background) preserves those conditions in the project's history.

PORTS-Pike's unusual scale begins with this brownfield inheritance. A normal data-center search looks for land, power, water, and community acceptance separately. Piketon's previous mission had already organized all four once before.

The historical 1,950 MW still cannot explain 8 IT-GW. Most of the capacity must be replicated from scratch.

## Why Does 10 GW Become Only 8 IT-GW?

[NVIDIA's announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-guarantees-sb-energy-s-ports-pike-technology-campus-in-ohio-to-exclusively-host-nvidia-ai-compute) places the two numbers together: at least 10 GW of new generation will support 8 IT-GW of AI-factory capacity. They sit at different boundaries of the power system.

![What PORTS-Pike's 10 GW, 8 IT-GW, and GPU Power Measure](/images/ports-pike-capacity-boundaries.en.svg)

Ten gigawatts sits on the generation side. Electricity still crosses transmission, substations, and campus distribution before reaching the facility's IT-power boundary. Cooling, pumps, fans, lighting, and security draw facility power. CPUs, networking, storage, and accelerators then divide IT power. The amount that reaches GPUs is smaller again.

Dividing ten by eight therefore does not prove a PUE of 1.25. Planned generation and the lease target remain separated by grid topology, reliability reserves, phase timing, and operating load curves. Public disclosures prove that PORTS-Pike includes its energy source in the program. They do not prove that power for 8 IT-GW is already available.

Likewise, 8 GW inside one agreement does not place every phase in the same construction state.

## Is the 8 GW One Project or a Portfolio of Projects?

PORTS-Pike spans adjacent private land and the DOE Portsmouth Site. The private-land FAST-41 scope covers roughly 1,300 acres, currently including two data center buildings plus roads, substations, and construction support. DOE land is released in batches after remediation. The [April 2026 Batch 1 document](https://www.energy.gov/nepa/articles/cx-270956-environmental-site-assessment-esa-batch-1-leased-property-portsmouth) confirms only about 189 acres entering Environmental Site Assessment.

![PORTS-Pike's Federal Land, Private Land, and Permitting Boundaries](/images/ports-pike-land-permitting.en.svg)

“One PORTS-Pike” is therefore a commercial and geographic boundary. In engineering terms it is a portfolio of phases with separate parcels, permits, buildings, substations, energization events, and acceptance dates. Groundbreaking can coexist with permit review; the first phase can enter Early Construction while later land remains under assessment.

That is the first reason 8 GW can be signed at once. A contract can define the final campus ceiling while land and engineering unfold by batch. OpenAI has secured a long-term supply path, not a completed design for every building.

Only the first 800 MW is close enough to have a visible delivery route.

## Why Is the First Batch Only 800 MW?

[OpenAI's delivery plan](https://openai.com/index/openai-joins-ports-pike-project/) divides the 8 GW into two paths. The first 800 MW is expected to begin becoming available in 2028, largely using existing AEP infrastructure. Later development requires new power plants, new transmission lines, and associated infrastructure.

![The Parallel Plan and Hard Dependencies for PORTS-Pike's First 800 MW](/images/ports-pike-first-phase-gantt.en.svg)

The 800 MW is not an arbitrary 10%. It approximates the capacity that can start through today's grid path, temporarily removing new generation and 765 kV transmission from the first phase's critical path. Site assessment, permit review, facility design, AEP service studies, long-lead equipment, and NVIDIA system planning can move in parallel before converging at Permanent Power, facility commissioning, and cluster acceptance.

Even then, 2028 is not the moment all 800 MW switch on. The official language says capacity begins coming online in phases in 2028. One building or data hall may pass while the rest remains in construction and commissioning.

The first 800 MW acts as a bridgehead. It proves whether the site can deliver facilities and clusters before the program builds a different power system for the remaining 7.2 GW.

## Why Isn't the Remaining 7.2 GW Nine Copies of the First Phase?

The first batch borrows existing AEP infrastructure. The remaining 7.2 GW must replicate infrastructure itself. [PORTS-Pike's disclosed plan](https://portscampus.com/) includes 10 GW of new generation, 9.2 GW of it natural gas, plus at least $4.2 billion of AEP Ohio 765 kV transmission.

![Why the First 800 MW and the Remaining 7.2 GW Follow Different Paths](/images/ports-pike-phase-split.en.svg)

Later phases must close four workstreams. Generation needs fuel supply, air permits, interconnection, major equipment, construction, and grid synchronization. Transmission needs system planning, routes, rights of way, siting, long-lead equipment, and line construction. Facilities need civil, MEP, and commissioning. Clusters then require NVIDIA deployment and OpenAI acceptance.

A finished power plant without transmission cannot deliver electricity. A completed line without a commissioned facility cannot create lease capacity. A delivered facility without an accepted cluster still leaves OpenAI without schedulable compute.

PORTS-Pike has not made data-center replication ten times faster. It has concentrated dependencies that used to be distributed across campuses, utility territories, and contracts into one program. Once the scale changes, the bottleneck moves from “where can power still be found?” to “can an entire power system be built on schedule?”

## Is Concentrating 8 GW Faster or More Fragile?

Concentration has a clear scale logic. Multiple phases can reuse the site team, Basis of Design, supplier frameworks, construction camps, network routes, and operations organization. Ten gigawatts of generation and 765 kV transmission do not need to be negotiated separately for each small campus. Once the first design and delivery cadence work, later buildings and clusters may become a genuine replication engine.

The same concentration amplifies interface risk. A delay in generation, transmission, fuel, permits, or a critical equipment chain can hit several phases together. One geography puts extreme weather, the regional grid, fiber routes, and community relations on the same project plan. An 8 GW build also competes internally for the same transformers, switchgear, turbines, craft labor, and commissioning resources.

That does not mean all 8 GW will share one unsegmented technical failure domain. Buildings, substations, fabrics, and clusters can be isolated. What becomes concentrated is **schedule and capital exposure**. A 765 kV route that slips one year can move several future MW batches, not one building.

PORTS-Pike trades portfolio scale for campus replication efficiency, then compresses the portfolio's distributed risks back into one master schedule.

## How Can 8 GW Be Signed Before It Is Built?

The second reason sits in the contract. SB Energy will build, own, and operate phases under a 20-year lease. OpenAI begins paying only when completed capacity becomes available for lease. The 8 GW defines the ultimate demand ceiling without triggering rent on all 8 GW at signing.

NVIDIA will invest $1.5 billion in SB Energy and provide Credit Support for Land, Power, and Shell associated with the initial 4.25 IT-GW, while retaining an option for the remaining 3.75 IT-GW. Even the capital commitment differs between the first and second halves.

![Cash-flow Gates from Purchase Order to Lease Payment](/images/ports-pike-payment-gates.en.svg)

The structure splits an apparently unfinanceable 8 GW headline into batches of cash-flow events. Equipment suppliers receive purchase orders after Design Freeze. EPC firms bill progress. AEP deploys transmission capital that may enter Rate Base. SB Energy starts rent at Facility Available. NVIDIA waits for shipment and acceptance. OpenAI ultimately recovers cost from production workloads.

Credit Support improves financing capacity. It does not guarantee delivery of 4.25 IT-GW. Permits, generation, transmission, facilities, and clusters still close one by one. **The 8 GW can be signed first because payment and risk are phased, not because supply has already been replicated.**

## Will the Full 8 GW Really Exist by 2032?

Public disclosures provide only two reliable anchors: the first 800 MW begins becoming available in 2028, and the six-year buildout continues through 2032. They do not disclose delivery in 2029, 2030, or 2031, nor Power Available, Facility Available, or Cluster Accepted for each phase.

![PORTS-Pike's Disclosed Capacity Anchors and Unknown Interval](/images/ports-pike-capacity-timeline.en.svg)

A solid line rising evenly between the two anchors would turn unknown generation, transmission, facility, and compute milestones into management guidance. Through 2032 defines a planned buildout window. It does not guarantee 8 IT-GW of healthy capacity on December 31, 2032.

The first and later phases need separate judgments. The first 800 MW has an existing AEP path, field activity, and a 2028 target, placing it on an execution route that can be checked. But permits, Permanent Power, commissioning, and cluster acceptance remain open. The remaining 7.2 GW has no disclosed annual phases and depends on new generation and 765 kV transmission that do not yet exist.

Current evidence supports “the first phase has entered an executable path,” not “the full 8 GW is locked for on-time delivery.”

## Has a Business This Large Already Been Priced In?

AEP provides the clearest public-market boundary. Management already includes the Piketon transmission opportunity in more than $10 billion of incremental capital line of sight. [AEP's second-quarter 2026 guidance](https://www.aep.com/news/stories/view/12135/) raised full-year operating EPS to $6.25-$6.55 and maintained growth above 9% from 2025 through 2030. [AEP Investor Relations](https://www.aep.com/investors/) showed a share price of $125.70 on August 20, 2026.

Dividing $125.70 by the $6.40 midpoint of EPS guidance gives roughly **19.6 times forward operating earnings**. Piketon already sits inside management's capital-growth narrative, and the market has received the disclosure. Treating the entire $4.2 billion transmission opportunity as undiscovered upside would be unjustified.

[AEP's price history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/aep/history/) provides a second test. The shares closed at $125.60 before the announcement and $126.53 on announcement day, a 0.74% gain. By August 20 they were back at $125.70, only 0.08% above the pre-announcement close. There was no sustained revaluation that can be isolated as PORTS-Pike, but that does not prove the market assigned the project zero value. Interest rates, regulation, and other utility earnings move AEP every day as well.

What cannot be calculated is how much of PORTS-Pike itself has been priced in. The missing bridge is:

`PORTS-Pike Rate Base × Allowed ROE × AEP Ownership / Cost Recovery × In-Service Timing → Incremental EPS`

AEP does not separately disclose the final project rate base, approved return, in-service schedule, or EPS contribution. The 19.6 times multiple values the whole company and cannot isolate data-center growth from other utility earnings. We can confirm that the project has entered growth expectations and that its announcement caused no identifiable sustained revaluation. We cannot determine how much value the shares had already assigned to it. **PORTS-Pike's standalone price-in remains unverified.**

## What Must the Next Announcement Prove?

PORTS-Pike has committed to annual progress reports. The next useful announcement will not repeat 8 GW. It will prove that the concentrated “one campus equals six” model has started to replicate.

![The Events That Can Change PORTS-Pike's Project State](/images/ports-pike-evidence-dashboard.en.svg)

For the first phase, watch FAST-41 permits, Batch 1 executable design, the AEP service plan, substation energization, long-lead purchase orders, building dry-in, commissioning reports, and the first available-for-lease MW. They determine whether 800 MW can begin arriving through existing infrastructure in 2028.

For expansion, watch gas-generation sites, air permits, fuel, and interconnection; the 765 kV route, rights of way, equipment orders, and construction; and whether NVIDIA converts its option for 3.75 IT-GW into a stronger capital commitment. They determine whether PORTS-Pike can move from one large contract into a campus that repeatedly delivers.

If any of them continues moving right, the damage is larger than one milestone. The concentrated model transmits the delay into several future MW batches, and the 8 GW expected by 2032 must move down with it.

## Why Is PORTS-Pike Larger Than Six Stargate Campuses?

Because the object being compared has changed.

The previous Stargate round searched across the country for sites that could deliver power and facilities quickly, assembling nearly 7 GW from several campuses. PORTS-Pike takes an industrial site that once consumed nearly 2 GW continuously for a national power-intensive mission, then binds 10 GW of generation, 765 kV transmission, facilities, NVIDIA compute, and a 20-year OpenAI lease into one program.

It is not one abnormally large data center. It is an industrial system attempting to replicate the full energy-to-compute stack in one location. Eight gigawatts can appear first because ultimate capacity is contracted while engineering, payment, and risk unfold by phase. Whether the 8 GW ultimately appears depends on converting the first 800 MW's site advantage into a replication advantage for the remaining 7.2 GW.

The problems previously distributed across six campuses have not disappeared. They have been compressed into one larger project. That is why the next bottleneck has already moved upstream from data center buildings into generation, transmission, permits, and project finance.
